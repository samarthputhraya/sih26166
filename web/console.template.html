Your own images

Looking for the registration server on this machine.

GeoTIFF, PDS (.img, .xml, .lbl), PNG or JPEG. The two must show overlapping ground. A GeoTIFF's map scale is used; a PNG or JPEG has none, so both are taken to be at about the same scale. The reference is matched as one window of at most 640 px: photos are reduced to fit, both by the same factor; crop a larger GeoTIFF reference first.

Give it any Moon picture. The server warps it by the amounts below, registers the warped copy back onto the original and measures how far off it lands. Because the right answer is known, this is a real error, not an estimate. It is the same picture against itself, so no Sun or sensor changes: it tests geometry, blur and noise. Images over 900 px are shrunk to 900 first, and the shifts are in those pixels.

    One site, every camera

    The problem statement names OHRC, TMC and IIRS. At 60.7°N we found an OHRC frame, a TMC-2 pass with its own IIRS strip, and an LRO image whose Suns all lie within a few degrees, and registered every leg. Choosing images by their Sun is part of the method.

    The loop compares two routes from OHRC to TMC-2, one through the LRO image and one direct. Agreement means the registrations are consistent with each other; it is not a measurement of absolute accuracy. The IIRS windows lie along the same TMC-2 pass, not on the OHRC frame itself.

    Same ground, two Suns

    At the lunar south pole the Sun never climbs high, so shadows run long and swing right round the compass. Every shadow edge a matcher could use moves, or turns inside out.

    Sun A is the OHRC frame's own, 7° above the horizon (from its PDS label). Drag Sun B round the compass. Relief and shadows from the LOLA 60 m elevation model; contours every 250 m.

    We registered one OHRC frame against 25 LRO NAC frames whose Suns differ from it by 3° to 153° in azimuth: 69 real windows. Wherever you put Sun B, the last line below is the one that matters.

    The Sun raised, not only turned

    The check, tested on purpose

    A failure detector is only worth something if its false alarms and its blind spot are measured. So we planted wrong answers of known size in 30 real windows and asked it.

    2 of 53right answers refused in the real Sun sweep, on windows nobody chose (both with the Suns 60° to 120° apart). The planted test's control, the right answer re-checked, is flagged 0 of 44 and 0 of 16 times.

    100%of planted 5 m shifts caught with the Suns within 10° (176 trials), and of 10 m shifts with them 132° to 174° apart. At 2 m or less almost nothing is caught: that is the floor.

    The usual test of a registration is its own residual: how well the matches fit the transform. On the same 1,056 planted wrong answers of 5 m or more, a residual threshold loose enough to accept every true registration flags none of them; the squares flag all 1,056. The matches fit a wrong answer perfectly, so only the pixels can say it is wrong.

    Visible against infrared, 16 TMC-2 to IIRS windows on two orbits: the right answer re-checked is never flagged; 86% of 5-pixel shifts caught and 99% of 10-pixel shifts (IIRS pixels, 74 to 84 m).

    A rotation or scale change about the centre leaves the centre still and moves the corners, so a verdict for the whole frame watches the wrong place: at 3 m of corner movement it refuses 0 of 120 trials, at 5 m only 15. The squares carry it instead.

      How it runs

      core/pipeline.py, on a laptop CPU with no discrete GPU: a median of 9.0 s per 640-pixel window over 89 OHRC to NAC windows.

      Both images go to one ground scale. Lighting is reduced to gradient orientation. LoFTR matches them densely, tiled, on the CPU; each match is refined to sub-pixel by correlation; MAGSAC++ rejects outliers and fits the transform; the inliers' spread over the 8×8 grid is measured. Then the independent area check cross-correlates the warped image against the reference, square by square, and never reads a match position. It is the only step allowed to overrule the others. If it agrees, the result ships as a GeoTIFF on the reference grid with control points for GDAL and QGIS (all of them, and a set thinned to at most 4 per square so they cover the image), an ISIS match list, the metrics and the trust map. If it is unconfirmed, it is kept and labelled. If it contradicts, the matcher's answer is refused and a phase-correlation fallback is delivered, declared as such, with its uncertainty in metres.

      Words we hold to

      Cross-sensor means different instruments. NAC with NAC, OHRC with OHRC, and TMC-2 fore with aft are the same sensor and are labelled so.

      Multi-modal means visible with infrared, or optical with elevation. Two panchromatic cameras are not.

      Sub-pixel always names its grid and gives metres.

      Accepted means the area check agrees with the matcher and no fallback was needed; unconfirmed, kept and labelled, not certified; refused, contradicted, and a declared fallback given instead. A square is verified when matches and pixels agree in it, weak when it was measured but the alignment is not confirmed there, no evidence when nothing could be measured.

      Reproduce every number

      The evidence pages read the evidence logs, whose rows were measured at the freeze commit . The saved results come from the pipeline's cached run of each pair, which can differ from the logged row by a few inliers because MAGSAC++ samples at random; the verdicts do not. The downloads are the frozen run's exported files.
